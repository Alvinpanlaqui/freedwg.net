Submersible Stormwater Pump Pit Section and Performance Curve CAD Drawing

Engineer reliable mechanical stormwater evacuation, automated float-switch staging, and rising main discharge for below-grade basements, sunken carparks, and low-lying building drainage systems with this comprehensive CAD cross-section of a dual submersible pump pit paired with a pump performance curve diagram. Specifically tailored for hydraulic engineers, MEP consultants, basement drainage designers, and civil draftsmen, this AutoCAD DWG drawing illustrates a reinforced concrete pump chamber equipped with duty/assist submersible pumps, non-return check valves, level control elevations, heavy-duty access hardware, and a gridded head-versus-flow performance chart. Whether you are designing council-compliant basement pump-out systems where gravity drainage is unachievable, specifying dual-pump redundancy for commercial developments, or preparing mechanical-hydraulic permit packages, this technical detail provides the exact operational parameters and civil-mechanical interfaces required for construction documentation.

On the left side of the sheet, the vertical section cut through the reinforced concrete sump pit details both the mechanical pumping hardware and the surrounding structural chamber. Topped by a 'CLASS B (HEAVY DUTY) HINGED GALVANISED MILD STEEL GRATE & FRAME, PROVIDE LOCKING DEVICE', the wet well features a recessed pump sump on the left and a sloped collection bench on the right with a Top Water Level (TWL) marked at 8.10 and an average storage depth of 0.79 m. Inside the deeper pump well, the mechanical arrangement is annotated as '2 AUTO SUBMERSIBLE PUMPS OPERATING SIMULTANEOUSLY ON HIGH LEVEL ALARMS AT 10 L/sec. PUMPS TO BE INSTALLED & SPECIFIED TO MANUFACTURERS DETAIL & CALC. SHEET'. Vertical float-switch control levels are clearly plotted along the left wall—indicating 'PUMP OFF', 'PUMP ON' (separated by a 300 mm operating differential), and the upper emergency stage where 'BOTH PUMPS ON & ALARM SOUNDS'. Each vertical discharge riser passes through a 'NON-RETURN FLAP VALVE' before connecting into a horizontal 'Ø65mm PVC (PUMP LINE CLASS 12 RISING MAIN)' exiting through the left concrete wall. A practical maintenance note at the bottom left specifies: 'OWNER TO MAINTAIN THIS AREA CLEAN REGULARLY FROM SILTATION EVERY 3-6 MONTHS'.

On the right side of the drawing, a companion 'PUMP PERFORMANCE CURVES' chart displays a structured grey grid overlaid with yellow system and pump operating curves, enabling engineers to verify duty points, total dynamic head, and flow rates directly alongside the pit section.

Drafted with crisp, color-coded vector layers that separate yellow concrete walls and performance curves from red level lines, leader callouts, and grey rising main piping, this detail block integrates seamlessly into hydraulic and MEP sheet sets without causing viewport lag. Designers and drafters can easily customize the pit depth, float elevations, rising main diameter, or duty flow rate to match project-specific hydraulic calculations and manufacturer pump selections. Fully compatible with Autodesk AutoCAD, AutoCAD LT, BricsCAD, ZWCAD, and all DWG-supporting CAD platforms, this drawing serves as a dependable reference for MEP, hydraulic, and basement infrastructure drafting libraries.
